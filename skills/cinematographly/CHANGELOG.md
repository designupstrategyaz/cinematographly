# Change list

One line per version, newest first.

| Version | Date | What changed |
|---|---|---|
| 3.1.2 | 2026-11-16 | Licence 1.0, clarified before launch (an IP review, not legal advice): a 30-day grace to finish a job when a studio grows past 10; sharing, hosting and selling spelled out; disclaimers in capitals with a liability cap; no patent licence; severability; your copy keeps the terms it came with; a changed version shared with others needs another name. Contributions confirm an employer's permission and include a patent licence. The repository now has a support link, issue forms, a pull-request template and a code of conduct; the README puts install first. |
| 3.1.1 | 2026-11-16 | The new-client interview takes the mode in words ("narrative promo", "UGC", "stills") and asks whether captions are burned in, so no placeholder is left in the profile. Doctor flags a client cost file that still holds the template's placeholder prices. The demo job is numbered 00000, so your first real job is 01001. `/cinematographly:connectors` names your Higgsfield connector; naming one `--other` through the plugin (which lets its generate tools through) now needs the person's own words naming that connector, and a connector the gate has seen serving a Higgsfield-only tool is refused. Hand edits and the connector environment variables are listed under "What the checks do not cover". |
| 3.1.0 | 2026-11-16 | Onboarding and production settings. Studio answers (`/cinematographly:studio`) pre-fill new clients and jobs; `/cinematographly:new-client` interviews you and writes a client profile; `/cinematographly:demo` makes a finished sample job to read; doctor gives a first-run checklist; your own rate readings live in `<studio>/rates-override.json` and survive updates. Production settings: an enforced monthly credit ceiling (a person confirms each call past it; raising it needs an approver's own words), regens per batch approval, a retake factor for new clients' estimates, an optional second-opinion model. Each window keeps its own job and transcript, and approvals are always checked against what the person wrote (`--hookless` only for Cowork). Review packs to show a client outside the chat; the studio's "no" on music or likeness blocks export; a scene that failed continuity gets no clip; `/cinematographly:deliver` builds upload-by-hand folders per platform; closing a job writes a scorecard. A budget of 0 now refuses paid generation outright. `gate.py status` prints every setting with its source. The ~1.7× clip length is the edit-handle rule, not a retake allowance. |
| 3.0.0 | 2026-11-15 | First public release. A director and producer for short-form AI video and stills inside Claude Code: development (your voice first, research, five concepts, kill rounds), script, storyboard and animatic, casting sheets, start frames, voice, clips with realism QA, music, a motion-driven cut plan, a timeline for DaVinci Resolve, Final Cut Pro (FCPXML 1.10) or Premiere Pro (xmeml, experimental), titles, mix, QC and export. A spend gate checks every paid call against an open job, the approvals before it in the person's own words, a linted prompt and a budget, and fails closed. First run: `/cinematographly:setup`, `doctor`, `new-job`, `next`. Licence: Cinematographly Studio License 1.0 — free for studios of 10 people or fewer, a commercial licence above. |
