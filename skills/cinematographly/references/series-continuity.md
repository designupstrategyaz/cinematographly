# Series continuity — documenting a recurring series across many videos

**When to read this:** a brand is on its third video with the same character and you cannot say what
is locked; episode 12 looks nothing like episode 1; you need to know which hooks have already run;
or a client asks what changed and when. `pm-workflow.md` runs a *project* — one video, its folders,
its board. **This runs the series above it.**

Checked **2026-08-29**. ⛔ Almost nothing here has a published standard; the two genuinely primary
documents available are named, and everything that circulates as a "template" online is commercial
content marketing from screenwriting-software businesses. Where a rule is this skill's invention it
says so.

---

## 0. Two documents, not one — and professional practice keeps them separate

| | The **bible** | The **continuity log** |
|---|---|---|
| Direction | **Prospective** — what will be true | **Retrospective** — what was actually done |
| Scope | Per **series** | Per **take / episode** |
| Answers | *What may not change?* | *What did we do, so the next thing can match?* |
| Owner in film | Showrunner / writers' room | Script supervisor |

Continuity documentation in film is per-take and retrospective; a bible is per-series and
prospective. **A recurring AI-generated series needs both, and they are different documents with
different owners.** Collapsing them is why "we have a brand guide" never prevents drift — a brand
guide is a bible with no log under it.

---

## 1. The bible — no standard exists, but two real ones do

**The series bible is universal professional practice with essentially no authoritative published
template.** [SECONDARY · encyclopaedic definition · checked 2026-08-29] The top search results for
"show bible template" are content marketing from script-coverage and screenwriting-software
companies — treat all of them accordingly.

The two documents worth learning from are primary and public:

- **The *Star Trek: The Next Generation* Writers'/Directors' Guide (23 March 1987)** — archived in
  full. [PRIMARY document located, contents not read · checked 2026-08-29]
- **The Smokey Bear Guidelines** (USDA Forest Service, Ad Council, National Association of State
  Foresters; first distributed 2004, current edition read August 2022) — a decades-maintained,
  publicly downloadable **character-consistency document**, and unusually explicit about rules rather
  than vibes. [PRIMARY · NASF-hosted official guidelines · checked 2026-08-29]

**What makes Smokey worth copying is its register.** It fixes appearance to the item (hat, belted
jeans, shovel; the costume check asks *"Is the hat crown up?"*), fixes the message
(*"Only You Can Prevent Wildfires"*, changed from "Forest Fires" in 2001), fixes personality
(*"dignified and friendly"*, remaining anonymous) — and then writes the **prohibitions** down as
plainly: never endorse a commercial product; *"should not act aggressively"*; *"avoid clowning,
horseplay, inappropriate dancing"*; *"never be photographed in less than full costume"*; and
⛔ **"Do NOT speak during costumed appearances."**

**The lesson: a usable bible is mostly a list of things that must not happen.** Positive description
("warm, approachable, trustworthy") does not constrain a generator. A prohibition does, and it can be
checked. ⛔ Smokey is atypical — a statutorily protected public-service character — and **no
commercial mascot guideline document is public.** Do not expect to find a competitor's.

## 2. ⛔ The distinction that matters most, and where it comes from

The writers'-bible tradition documents *what is true* about the world. It is not the tradition this
pipeline resembles. **A recurring ad series is a repeatable machine run many times with local
variation — which is a format, not a show** — and exactly one tradition writes the constant/variable
split down explicitly.

The **TV format bible**, from the licensed-format business. Its stated core requirement: bibles must

> *"clarify from the get-go which format elements are essential to the show's mechanics — and
> therefore non-negotiable — and which are non-essential."*

[PRIMARY for the author's claim about his own template · The Format People, on the FRAPA Format Bible
Template (rev. 2021) · checked 2026-08-29 · ⛔ the template itself is members-only and was not read]

And the reason it is load-bearing rather than tidy: FRAPA's **Format Analysis Service** exists to
establish whether two formats are *"essentially the same"*, producing written statements usable in
legal proceedings. [PRIMARY · FRAPA · checked 2026-08-29] **In format licensing, the list of
non-negotiable elements is effectively the definition of the intellectual property.**

For a brand that is building a recurring character, that is the right level of seriousness: **the
invariant list is the asset.** Everything else is this month's execution.

## 3. The three-way split — INVARIANT / VARIANT / DRIFT

⛔ **This three-way version is this skill's, not received practice.** The format tradition gives two
buckets (essential / non-essential). A generative pipeline needs a third, because it has a failure
mode the format business does not: things that change *without anyone deciding they should*.

| Class | Definition | A change is… | Enforcement |
|---|---|---|---|
| **INVARIANT** | Identity, voice, the offer, approved claims, the delivery format, the compliance line | **a defect** | The client profile's ⛔ hard locks; `realism-qa.md` V12/V13 (identity and product drift) |
| **VARIANT** | Framing distance, angle, exact duration, hook, persona, environment, palette-within-the-system | **required** | `realism-qa.md` V14 — identical framing and duration across a batch is itself an AI tell, and platforms demote template output |
| **DRIFT** | Anything that differs and was in neither list | **the thing to catch** | Caught by comparing an episode against the invariant list; every drift is either promoted to VARIANT or fixed |

**The point of the third column: a series needs consistency *and* freshness simultaneously**, and
without naming both, a producer defending consistency kills the variance the platform rewards, and a
producer chasing freshness breaks the identity lock. Most series documents name only the first.

⛔ **Every drift is a decision, not a mistake to erase.** When you find one, it goes to
`_series/DECISIONS.md` as either *"promoted to VARIANT on <date> because…"* or *"defect, corrected in
ep N"*. That is how the invariant list stays honest instead of becoming aspirational.

## 4. Which layer to vary — the one measured answer

The format tradition fixes *mechanics* and frees everything else. The advertising literature says the
correct fixed layer depends on the audience, which is a different claim.

**Two kinds of variation, defined:** *cosmetic* variation changes non-essential visual elements —
endorsers, fonts, layout, colours, background — while the core message stays identical. *Substantive*
variation changes message content and product arguments while the visual design stays consistent.

Findings, from the hypotheses confirmed:

> *"Cosmetic variation in repeated ads will have a greater impact on attitudes when the product is of
> low rather than high personal relevance"* · *"Substantive variation… when the product is of high
> rather than low personal relevance"*

[PRIMARY · Schumann, Petty & Clemons (1990), *Journal of Consumer Research* 17(2), two experiments ·
checked 2026-08-29]

**Read against this pipeline:** a low-involvement category (a snack, a homeware impulse buy) wants the
message held and the surface varied. A high-involvement one (a solar installation, a supplement
someone will actually swallow, anything with a grant application behind it) wants the **surface held
and the argument varied** — which is the opposite of what a hook-testing batch does by default.

⛔ **Contradiction, left standing.** The format-bible convention fixes mechanics and frees the rest;
Schumann et al. imply the correct fixed layer is **audience-dependent**, not fixed by genre. Neither
source acknowledges the other, and this skill does not resolve it. What it does is force the question
into the open: **write down which layer you are holding and why**, in `_series/INVARIANTS.md`.

## 5. Which elements belong on the invariant list

Not the ones you are fond of. The rigorous criterion is a brand-asset one: assets — colours,
characters, sounds, taglines — are scored on **Fame** (how many people link the asset to *something*)
and **Uniqueness** (how many link it to *your brand and no other*), and plotted on a grid to decide
which to protect, which to invest in, and which to abandon. [PRIMARY for the citation, the two metrics
and the grid's existence · Romaniuk, *Building Distinctive Brand Assets*, OUP 2018 (Ehrenberg-Bass) ·
⛔ verified from the publisher's catalogue page; **the book was not read** and the page does not explain
how the metrics combine · checked 2026-08-29]

**The usable half without the book:** an element earns INVARIANT status when changing it would cost
recognition — not when it merely looks nice. For a young series nothing has Fame yet, so the honest
default is: **lock identity and compliance, leave the rest VARIANT until performance data says an
element is doing work** (`performance-metrics.md`).

**Is a recurring character worth it at all?** The strongest available evidence, with a flag: against
400 IPA Databank case studies since 1998, campaigns with characters showed new-customer gains 40.9%
vs 32.0%, market-share gains 40.9% vs 29.7%, profit gains 34.1% vs 26.2% — and only 4% of US and 7%
of UK ads used brand characters. ⛔ **The cut was chosen and presented by a vendor that sells
character testing**, and its accompanying emotional-response star ratings are that vendor's
proprietary metric; the Databank half is the stronger part because the dataset is independent even
though the cut was not. [SECONDARY · trade reporting of a System1 presentation · checked 2026-08-29]

## 6. The artifacts — what to actually keep

A `_series/<BRAND>/` folder alongside the per-project folders (`pm-workflow.md`). Six files, each
with one job. ⛔ **Keep them short enough to be read before an episode, or they will not be.**

```
_series/<BRAND>/
  BIBLE.md          the world, the characters, the voice — and mostly the PROHIBITIONS (§1)
  INVARIANTS.md     the three-way split (§3), versioned; the layer being held and why (§4)
  EPISODES.md       the registry: one row per episode (§6.1)
  DECISIONS.md      dated decision records — every T3 research call, every drift ruling (§3)
  HOOKS-USED.md     which hooks have run, on which episode, with the result
  _reference/       the locked reference images: character / wardrobe / props / environment / light
  BATCH-<ID>/       one folder per episode BATCH in flight (section 6.2) — the durable state that
                    lets several episodes run at once and survive a context reset
```

`_reference/` is the same folder the generation stage already reads, and its slot layout maps onto
the reference budget of the model roster (`prompting-craft.md` §6b) — which is what makes "one
reference carries one attribute" a series-level rule rather than tidiness.

`scripts/series_registry.py init --brand <BRAND>` scaffolds all six with their instructions inside;
`series_registry.py check _series/<BRAND>/` validates the structure — required columns filled, the
invariants version declared, episodes on a stale version listed, hook reuse flagged, and join keys
checked for the five-part shape the metrics layer joins on. ⛔ **It never looks at a frame**: identity
and product drift stay with the QA card and the eye.

### 6.1 The episode registry

One row per episode, written when the episode ships, not when it is planned:

| Ep | Date | Hook ID | Persona | Invariants version | Ad-name join key | Aliases | Notes / drift found |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

**`Ep` is the canonical show id — `<brand>-<SS><EEE>`, e.g. `acme-01007`** (brand `acme`, season
`01`, episode `007`): the episodic form published in **ETC/MovieLabs *VFX Image Sequence Naming*
v1.0**. It is allocated here when the episode is planned, it is the prefix on every generated file
for that episode, and ⛔ it never changes. The shape is deliberate: fixed-width so a parser is three
lines, and **allocatable without coordination**, which a flat counter is not — two parallel branches
will race a counter (`pm-workflow.md` § Naming convention). The **human label** (`ACME S01E007`) is
what people say and what the board shows; only the filename form is a key.

**`Aliases`** carries every earlier name for the episode — the `Proiect-Video-7` era and the
short-lived `ACME-S01E07` shape both. ⛔ Old folders are **not renamed**: a rename breaks every board link, manifest
path and cost record pointing at them, and buys nothing the alias column doesn't.

**The `Invariants version` column is the one that earns its keep.** When the bible changes, episodes
made under the old version are not wrong — they are *dated*, and the column tells you which ones
would need re-cutting if the change is retroactive. Without it, a bible edit silently reclassifies
every past episode as non-compliant.

The **ad-name join key** ties the registry to performance (`performance-metrics.md`) — the registry
is where "which creative was this?" gets answered, and it is the only place the creative record and
the media record meet. ⛔ **The two keys are deliberately different and must stay so:** an ad name
cannot change once the ad is live (a rename orphans its history), while the episode ID is assigned
before any ad exists. Merging them would make one of the two unstable. The registry row is the join.

### 6.2 ⛔ The freeze — what has to be true before several episodes run at once

Episodes are normally made two to five at a time (`series-parallel.md`). That is only safe because of
a property this file establishes and nothing else does: **the locked reference is the lock, not the
previous episode's output.** Three branches generating from the same frozen `_reference/` cannot
drift *relative to each other*, because none of them is downstream of another.

Break the freeze and the property inverts. A bible still moving while three branches are in flight
does not parallelise production — **it parallelises the drift**, and the bill arrives at the join,
after the credits are spent.

**So the freeze is a gate, not a habit:**

- [ ] `INVARIANTS.md` declares `invariants-version: N` and nothing is unresolved in `DECISIONS.md`
- [ ] `_reference/` holds the locked images, one attribute per reference (`prompting-craft.md` §6b)
- [ ] `series_registry.py check` exits 0
- [ ] every branch stamps that version — `series_batch.py init` refuses to open a batch otherwise,
      and `series_join.py` fails any branch whose stamp does not match

⛔ **A bible edit mid-batch does not apply retroactively and does not apply silently.** It bumps the
version, which makes every in-flight branch *stale* — dated, not wrong, exactly as §6.1 says of past
episodes — and the join reports it rather than absorbing it. That is the whole reason the version is
a column and not a note.

## 7. The per-episode continuity log

What a script supervisor tracks, and what an AI pipeline can and cannot inherit. The film role
produces a **lined script**, a **daily editor's log** (every take, its duration, notes on the action)
and **continuity logs** to editorial, covering: action and screen direction · dialogue and whether it
was captured on camera · wardrobe, hair and makeup · props and set dressing · camera details ·
eyelines and the axis. [SECONDARY · encyclopaedic description of the role, corroborated by a film-school
production handbook · PRIMARY · IATSE Local 871, for the role's scope · checked 2026-08-29]

**What transfers directly:** wardrobe/props/set state, eyelines and the axis, and screen direction —
all already in the blocking card (`direction.md` §2) and the shot manifest (`shot_manifest.py`).
**The manifest is this pipeline's continuity log**, and its discipline — a retake is a new row
carrying the one variable that changed — is the script supervisor's discipline in another medium.

⛔ **What does not transfer: nothing here records what the model actually did.** A script supervisor
observes the take; a generator returns a file. The gap is closed only by measurement
(`scripts/clip_qa.py`, `export_qc.py`) and by the eye checks the QA card mandates — which is why the
QA card is part of continuity, not a separate concern.

## 8. Running the series, month to month

Three PM-plugin skills do jobs this skill has been improvising:

- **`product-management:roadmap-update`** — the **slate**. Now / Next / Later across a brand's
  episodes, and the discipline of saying *what moves out* when something moves in. A video series is
  a roadmap with a smaller unit.
- **`product-management:stakeholder-update`** — the **client update**, tailored by audience and
  cadence. `review-and-approval.md` covers sign-off on a *file*; this covers the narrative around it,
  which is a different artifact and the one that keeps a retainer.
- **`product-management:sprint-planning`** — the **batch**, sized against real availability. Pair it
  with `batch_plan.py --hours` so the capacity number is the measured one (6.5h per setup) rather
  than an estimate.
- **`product-management:metrics-review`** for the monthly read — but the diagnosis waterfall and the
  join key stay in `performance-metrics.md`, which is more specific and already sourced.

⛔ **Do not let a PM artifact replace a locked one.** A roadmap can move an episode; it cannot move a
compliance gate, an approved script, or an invariant. The client profile's hard locks outrank every
document in this file.

## 9. What this file cannot tell you

- **There is no published series-bible standard.** Not from a guild, a broadcaster, a film body or a
  funder. What exists is trade practice, two good primary examples, and a lot of content marketing.
- **The FRAPA template was not read** — members-only. Everything about its internal structure comes
  from FRAPA's and its author's own descriptions of it.
- **No commercial mascot guideline is public**, so the only worked example of character governance
  available is a government one with statutory backing behind it.
- **Romaniuk's grid is cited, not read** — the metrics' names and the framework are verified; how they
  combine is not.
- **The character-effectiveness numbers are a vendor's chosen cut**, even though the underlying
  dataset is independent.
- **The three-way INVARIANT / VARIANT / DRIFT split is this skill's invention.** It rests on a real
  two-way distinction from format licensing plus this pipeline's own tell (V14). It has not been
  tested against a series that ran long enough to prove it.
- **Nothing here is measured on this account's own series yet.** The first dialogue series this was written for is the first
  candidate, and until a registry has run for a few months the whole file is a design, not a finding.
