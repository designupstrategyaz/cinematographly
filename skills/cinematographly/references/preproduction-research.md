# Pre-production research — the method, and how deep to go

**When to read this:** you are about to research an audience, an offer, a category or a claim before
a brief or a script is locked; you cannot tell whether a question deserves ten minutes or a day; or
you have researched something thoroughly and cannot say what decision it changed.
`research-quality.md` governs **how a claim is sourced and labelled** — tiers, decay, the refutation
pass. This governs **which questions get asked at all, and how hard.** They are different jobs and
this file does not repeat that one.

Checked **2026-08-29**. ⛔ Much of what advertising teaches about briefs is *convention retold as
law*; every place that is true below is marked, because the difference decides how hard you are
allowed to argue for it.

---

## 0. The organising principle

> **Information that cannot change which alternative you pick has a value of zero, however
> interesting it is.**

That is the operative implication of **Value of Information** — what a decision maker should be
willing to pay for information *before* deciding; never negative, bounded above by the value of
clairvoyance. [PRIMARY citation located, original not read · Howard, R.A., *Information Value
Theory*, IEEE Trans. Systems Science and Cybernetics (1966) · SECONDARY · Wikipedia "Value of
information" · checked 2026-08-29]

Everything in this file follows from it. Before researching anything, name the decision it feeds and
the alternatives it chooses between. **If you cannot name a decision that would come out differently,
you are not researching — you are reading**, and §4 says that is not merely wasteful but actively
distorting.

## 1. The brief

### 1.1 The six fields that are actually standard

Two bodies with no shared authorship — a UK commercial trade-body consortium and the UK
government — converge on the same skeleton. **This is the defensible core:**

| Field | IPA / ISBA / MCCA / PRCA | GCS OASIS |
|---|---|---|
| Current situation, the problem | *"Where are we now?"* | context to Objectives |
| Objective, stated measurably | *"Where do we want to be?"* | Objectives (SMART) |
| Audience | *"Who do we need to talk to?"* | Audience and insights |
| Strategy — what we will do | *"What are we doing to get there?"* | Strategy and ideas |
| Success measure, **defined in advance** | *"How will we know we've arrived?"* | Scoring and evaluation |
| Constraints, budget, approvals | *"Practicalities"*, *"Approvals"* | Implementation |

[PRIMARY · *The Client Brief: A best practice guide to briefing communications agencies*, IPA/ISBA/
MCCA/PRCA — read on a third-party mirror; the document references 2002 research and circulates in a
2006 edition, and it is **roughly twenty years old** · checked 2026-08-29] · [PRIMARY · UK Government
Communication Service, *Guide to campaign planning: OASIS*, 2020 · checked 2026-08-29]

⛔ **Everything beyond those six is convention, not specification.** The "single-minded proposition",
"consumer insight", "reason to believe", "tone of voice", "the one thing we want them to
think/feel/do" — no independent published standard mandates any of them. Use them because they help,
not because they are required, and never tell a client they are an industry standard.

⛔ **The single-minded proposition is untraceable.** No originator, agency, date or founding document
could be located — not even by the person who runs SMP workshops and wrote the how-to. Its nearest
documented ancestor is Rosser Reeves' **Unique Selling Proposition** (*Reality in Advertising*, 1961),
which is a narrower and different idea routinely conflated with it. What trade bodies actually endorse
is a single-minded **objective** — *"a single-minded and measurable objective is usually a
pre-requisite for success"* — which is a different artifact. **Single-mindedness is a discipline for
the brief writer, not a law of persuasion.**

**One house line on top of the six: the client's own sentences.** Before development starts, the
client writes three things in their own words — the opening, a plain description of the offer (what
the buyer actually gets), and the ask — and the brief carries those sentences as written. It claims
no standard behind it; it is §1.2's *"do not use the creative process to work out your strategy"*
applied to a real job, where a draft that never says what the offer is cannot be fixed by drafting
again. [PRACTITIONER · course notes] the homework is put at both half an hour and an hour; both are recorded,
neither is a default.

### 1.2 What a brief must not do — sourced

Sloppy briefing and moving goalposts · drowning the reader in contradictory information · vague terms
like *"improve brand image"* with no concrete detail · over-length (*briefs should be brief, not
exhaustive*) · woolly intermediate objectives standing in for concrete business objectives.
[PRIMARY · IPA/ISBA/MCCA/PRCA · checked 2026-08-29]

And the behavioural one, which is the failure mode of this pipeline specifically: ⛔ **do not use the
creative process to work out your strategy.** Six in ten marketers admit doing exactly that.
[SECONDARY · trade reporting of the BetterBriefs research · checked 2026-08-29] Generating footage to
discover what the ad is about is the most expensive possible way to think.

### 1.3 ⛔ Do not overclaim the evidence

The industry's own evidence that better briefs produce better outcomes is **perception data, not
outcome data.** BetterBriefs surveyed 1,700+ marketers and agency people across ~60–70 markets and
found a large perception gap — 80% of marketers think they write good briefs, 10% of agencies agree;
78% think their briefs give clear strategic direction, 5% agree. Waste is *self-estimated* at 30%
(marketers) and 37% (agencies). [SECONDARY · trade reporting · PRIMARY · IPA, for the UK cut ·
checked 2026-08-29]

**The widely-quoted $200bn figure is that self-estimate applied to global ad spend.** No campaign
outcome, sales result or effectiveness score is measured anywhere in it. A search for IPA Databank
analysis coding *brief quality* against effectiveness outcomes found none — the Databank's published
cuts code brand-vs-activation split, duration, emotional vs rational strategy and creative awards,
not briefs. [Negative finding · checked 2026-08-29]

**So: "good briefs drive effectiveness" is a working assumption supported by industry self-report.**
Act on it. Never cite it as proven, and never repeat the $200bn number without saying what it is.

### 1.3b ⛔ The seventh field: WHERE it goes out

The six standard fields describe the message and the audience. None of them names **the account that
will publish it** — and that is the largest term in the outcome that anyone can know before shipping.

- Acoustic features predicted song success at 0.74; adding **one artist-reputation variable** took it
  to **0.86** [PRIMARY · Interiano et al., *R. Soc. Open Sci.* 2018 · 500,000+ songs].
- The strongest single predictor of news popularity was **the publication source**, not the writing
  [PRIMARY · Bandari, Asur & Huberman 2012].
- Follower count is the most powerful predictor of TikTok virality [PRIMARY · Ling et al., ACM WebSci
  2022] — see `hook-craft.md` §4.

So the brief carries, as a required field:

> **Distribution:** which account publishes this · its audience and rough size · **the format this
> account already wins with** · whether this piece fits that format or deliberately breaks it.

A brief without it has omitted the biggest variable and left the strategy to be reverse-engineered
from the creative. And "deliberately breaks it" is a legitimate answer — it just has to be a decision
somebody made rather than one nobody noticed. What the field must **not** become is a forecast:
`performance-metrics.md` §0 has the ceilings, and `claims_lint.py` blocks the language.

### 1.4 Two fields to steal from the PM plugin

The video-brief convention has no equivalent of these, and both prevent the failures this pipeline
actually has. From `product-management:write-spec`:

- **Non-goals** — three to five things this video explicitly will *not* do, each with a one-line
  reason. This is the field that stops a 40-second ad from acquiring a second message in week two,
  and it is the cheapest revision control there is (`revisions.md`).
- **Open questions**, each tagged with **who answers it** and whether it is **blocking or
  non-blocking**. A blocking question is a stop; a non-blocking one is a note. Most video projects
  discover the difference during generation, which is the expensive place to discover it.

Route to that skill when writing the brief. Do not re-derive its structure here.

## 2. The depth ladder — the instrument this file exists for

**Four traditions, none invented for advertising, converge on the same structure.** Reversibility
sets how much rigour; VOI sets which questions are worth asking; the decision-quality chain says
over-investing in one link buys nothing if another is weak; and there is a published precedent for
declaring reduced rigour openly.

**Reversibility — the sorting question.** *"Some decisions are consequential and irreversible or
nearly irreversible – one-way doors – and these decisions must be made methodically, carefully,
slowly… But most decisions aren't like that – they are changeable, reversible – they're two-way
doors."* And the named failure: applying the one-way process to two-way decisions produces
*"slowness, unthoughtful risk aversion, failure to experiment sufficiently."* [PRIMARY · Amazon 2015
Letter to Shareholders · checked 2026-08-29]

**The stopping threshold.** *"Most decisions should probably be made with somewhere around 70% of the
information you wish you had. If you wait for 90%, in most cases, you're probably being slow."*
[PRIMARY · Amazon **2016** Letter to Shareholders · checked 2026-08-29] ⛔ **Citation note:** the 70%
rule and disagree-and-commit are in the **2016** letter; the doors are in the **2015** letter. The
2015 letter contains neither. This is a commonly mangled citation — get it right.

**The chain.** Decision quality has six requirements — right frame, creative alternatives, meaningful
and reliable information, clear values and trade-offs, sound reasoning, commitment to action — and
*"the overall quality of a decision can be no better than the weakest of these six."* [PRIMARY ·
Spetzler, Winter & Meyer, *Decision Quality*, Wiley 2016 / Strategic Decisions Group · ⛔ SDG is a
consultancy selling this method; the six-element list is their framework, not an independent finding ·
checked 2026-08-29] **Consequence: research is one link. A brilliantly-researched brief with no
alternatives considered is not a better decision.**

**The precedent for doing less on purpose.** A **Rapid Evidence Assessment** is a published protocol
for deliberately reduced rigour: *"in order to be 'rapid', an REA makes concessions in relation to the
breadth, depth and comprehensiveness of the search"* — and it states the price: *"an REA is more prone
to selection bias than a systematic review."* [PRIMARY · CEBMa, *Guideline for Rapid Evidence
Assessments*, eds. Barends, Rousseau & Briner, 2017 · checked 2026-08-29] **The transferable move is
not the shortcut; it is declaring the shortcut and naming the bias you accepted.**

### 2.1 The ladder

Pick the tier from the *decision*, not from how interesting the topic is.

| Tier | Use when | Depth | What it emits |
|---|---|---|---|
| **T0 · None** | Two-way door · one video · the fact decays before it matters · no alternative would change | Decide and move | A line in the brief saying the call was made without research, and on what basis |
| **T1 · Orientation** | Two-way door but you are unfamiliar · a convention you need to meet or break on purpose | ~15–30 min, 3–6 sources, no refutation pass | 3–8 bullets in the brief, each tied to a decision |
| **T2 · Structured** | The decision touches a **series**, a persona, or the offer · reversible but expensive to reverse | Saturation-guided (§3), tiers labelled, contradictions kept | A synthesis with themes and named sources (`product-management:synthesize-research`) |
| **T3 · Load-bearing** | ⛔ **One-way door**: a claim that will be spoken on camera · a compliance question · anything baked into a batch of 30–50 · a standing rule for a brand | T2 **plus the refutation pass** (`research-quality.md` §4) and primary sources only for the load-bearing claim | A decision record with the counter-case stated, in `_series/DECISIONS.md` |

### 2.1b The ladder written down — research that changes a decision (v2.69.0)

These rules hold for any step that turns research into a decision (a film, a product spec, a pitch, a campaign);
the plugin checks them in `00-brief/DEVELOPMENT.md` (`develop.py`, template 2.69+). They replace the old floor of
six sourced lines, which counted lines and never asked whether one changed anything.

1. **The tier is written first, from the decision.** `tier: T0|T1|T2|T3`, with the two answers below.
2. **T0 is one line:** the call, what it rests on, what would raise it to T1. Nothing else is owed.
3. **T1 and up: each line has an id, a source tag and the alternative it decides** —
   `R2 [PRIMARY · source · date] <fact> · decides: <C2 instead of C4 / rules out …>`. T1 is 3–8 lines. T2 adds a
   synthesis (themes, contradictions kept, named sources); T3 adds the counter-case (`research-quality.md` §4).
4. **Concepts cite the lines they rest on** (`research: R2`); evidence that agreed and changed nothing is cited
   as `confirms: R4`, so it is visible on the board. Only the person's naming keeps a line; a line they name must be
   cited by the chosen option or named in a kill, because that is where it landed.
5. **The person names what mattered.** Show each option with the lines it cites beside it, and ask which facts
   changed the decision. Their answer is recorded (`research that mattered: R2, R5`); every line they did not name
   is deleted and listed (`deleted: R4`). A writer cannot satisfy this alone, which is the point (§7).
6. **At T2+, one real customer phrase reaches the output.** Phrases are kept word for word, 3–15 words, with
   their kind (problem, objection, result, other), source type and date, never a customer's name. One problem or
   objection phrase is spoken in the chosen opening (a film with no spoken line carries it on screen); a result
   phrase is never spoken by any generated character. A real customer saying their own result in their own
   footage is a testimonial and goes through `compliance.md` §2, outside this check.

**The two questions that assign the tier**, in order:

1. **How many videos does being wrong touch?** One is a two-way door. Thirty is not.
2. **Can it be reversed after delivery?** A claim in a shipped, paid-media ad cannot. That is T3
   regardless of everything else, because the cost is not creative — it is legal (`compliance.md`).

⛔ **T0 is a legitimate answer and should be used often.** The default in this pipeline should be T0
or T1; T3 is rare by construction. A skill that researches everything to T3 has misread the
reversibility of its own work, and pays for it in the currency that matters here — the six hours a
video actually takes.

## 3. When to stop — saturation, and its critics

**The numbers, for a homogeneous audience and a narrow question.** Saturation occurred *"within the
first twelve interviews, although basic elements for metathemes were present as early as six."*
[PRIMARY · Guest, Bunce & Johnson (2006), *Field Methods* 18(1):59–82, n = 60 · checked 2026-08-29]
The systematic review puts it at **9–17 interviews** or **4–8 focus groups** — ⛔ **explicitly only
for relatively homogeneous populations with narrowly defined objectives**; heterogeneous or
multi-market designs need more. [PRIMARY · Hennink & Kaiser (2022), *Social Science & Medicine* 292,
23 articles reviewed · checked 2026-08-29] As few as **four** in highly homogeneous contexts, and one
study found **80% of themes within two to three focus groups**. [PRIMARY · a cardiovascular-nursing
review citing them · checked 2026-08-29]

**The distinction that matters: code saturation ≠ meaning saturation.** No *new codes* appearing is a
much earlier point than no new *dimensions or nuances* of those codes. [Hennink, Kaiser & Marconi 2017,
via secondary summaries] Roughly: themes ~9–13 interviews, **meaning ~24**.

⛔ **Three reasons saturation may not be your stopping rule:**

1. **Logical.** Saturation as "no new information" is *"a logical fallacy, as there are always new
   theoretical insights to be made as long as data continues to be collected and analysed."* Braun &
   Clarke recommend **avoiding claims of saturation altogether**. [PRIMARY for their position, from
   their own site; SECONDARY for the quotation · full paper not read, paywalled · checked 2026-08-29]
2. **Procedural.** Saturation is routinely *rubber-stamped* — invoked with no operational definition
   and no inductive process behind it. [PRIMARY · Kaiser, UCSD seminar slides · checked 2026-08-29]
3. **Purposive.** For evaluation-type questions — *how is this specific thing performing* rather than
   *what is generally true* — saturation is not the goal and a sample as low as one can be justified.
   [SECONDARY · checked 2026-08-29] **Most questions in this pipeline are of this kind.**

**The replacement worth using: information power.** Set the sample *before* you start, from five
dimensions — narrowness of aim, participant specificity, use of established theory, quality of
dialogue, analysis strategy. Narrower and more homogeneous → fewer needed. [Malterud, Siersma &
Guassora (2016), *Qualitative Health Research* 26(13) · SECONDARY for the five · checked 2026-08-29]

**House rule.** For a T2 pass on one persona in one market: **six sources for the basics, a dozen
before claiming you have the themes**, and say "themes stabilised" rather than "saturation reached."
For anything spanning segments or markets, that band is not yours.

## 4. ⛔ The cost of over-research — measured, not asserted

This section exists because the failure it describes feels like diligence.

**More information raises confidence, not accuracy.** 32 judges read a case in four cumulative
stages. **Accuracy 26.0% → 21.8%** — *"no significant increase in accuracy at all with increasing
information."* **Confidence 33.2% → 52.8%**, a *"striking and extremely significant rise"*;
overconfidence widened from 7 to 25 points (p < .001). [PRIMARY · Oskamp (1965), *Overconfidence in
case-study judgments* · checked 2026-08-29] Replicated in a modern paradigm: *"when judges receive
more information, their confidence increases more than their accuracy."* [PRIMARY · Tsai, Klayman &
Hastie (2008), *OBHDP* 107(2):97–105 · checked 2026-08-29]

**And the sharpest finding in this file: pursuing information you did not need changes your answer.**
People pursue *non-instrumental* information — information that, handed to them free, would not have
changed their choice — and once pursued it **becomes** instrumental and shifts the decision. Measured,
including with real money:

| Decision | Without pursuit | After pursuing | |
|---|---|---|---|
| Course registration — reject | 18% | **29%** | |
| Admissions — reject | 43% | **54%** | |
| Mortgage — approve | 29% | **56%** | |
| CD player — purchase | 91% | **55%** | |
| Raffle — participate | 57% | **23%** | real stakes |
| Ultimatum game — reject | 20% | **35%** | real money |

[PRIMARY · Bastardi & Shafir (1998), *On the pursuit and misuse of useless information*, JPSP ·
checked 2026-08-29] Mechanism: pursuit raises the weight given to the information, and people read
their own decision to wait as evidence that the answer must matter.

**This is the empirical case for T0.** Deciding not to research is not laziness; researching a
question whose answer cannot change the choice measurably degrades the choice.

⛔ **The contradiction, left unresolved.** Fast strategic decision makers used **more** information
and considered **more** alternatives than slow ones — and outperformed them. The differences were in
*kind* and *timing*: real-time operating metrics rather than planning forecasts, and alternatives
considered **in parallel** rather than sequentially. [PRIMARY · Eisenhardt (1989), *AMJ* 32(3):543–576,
eight microcomputer firms · checked 2026-08-29] The plausible bridge — **flagged as inference, not a
finding, and not drawn by any of these authors** — is that Eisenhardt's teams consumed data that
updates and can change action immediately, where the lab studies supplied static background. That maps
onto VOI, which is suspicious in a satisfying way. Treat it as a hypothesis.

**What survives both sides:** more *alternatives* is reliably good; more *background* is not. When in
doubt, spend the hour generating a third option rather than a fourth source on the first two.

## 5. What to research at brief stage, with its tier attached

| Question | Default tier | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **The offer and its substantiation** — what is actually true about the product | **T3** if a claim will be spoken; T1 otherwise | One-way door: `compliance.md`, and it is the client's liability as well as yours |
| **The audience's own words** — objections, phrasing, what they call the problem | **T2** | Feeds the hook bank and the persona; touches every video in the batch |
| **Category conventions** — what every competitor ad in this vertical does | **T1** | You need to know the convention to meet it or break it on purpose (`style-transfer.md`) |
| **Platform format and policy** | **T1**, re-checked at point of use | Decays fast (`research-quality.md` §5) |
| **The craft question** ("how do I get X on screen") | **T0–T1** | Usually already answered in this skill; check here first |
| **A standing rule for the brand** | **T3** | It is baked into every future video by definition |

⛔ **Competitor ad teardowns are a real T1–T2 instrument and this pipeline under-uses them.** Use
`product-management:competitive-brief` for the method — particularly its **message architecture** and
**positioning gap** analysis, which transfer directly: what claim does every competitor make, what
does none of them make, and is the gap empty because it is valuable or because it is illegal.

## 6. Turning research into something usable

Use `product-management:synthesize-research` for the method rather than improvising one. Its parts
that transfer without modification:

- **Thematic analysis** — familiarisation → generous initial coding → candidate themes → review
  against the data → name each theme in one or two sentences → report with evidence.
- **Affinity mapping** — one observation per note, cluster without pre-defined categories, label,
  and ⛔ *"outliers are interesting. Do not force every observation into a cluster."* In a hook bank
  the outlier is often the hook.
- **Triangulation** — methodological, source, temporal. And the disposition that matters:
  ⛔ **"When sources disagree, that is interesting"** — check whether different populations were
  measured, whether stated preference differs from actual behaviour, and **report the disagreement
  rather than choosing a side.** That is the same rule as `research-quality.md`'s "keep the
  contradiction", arriving from a different discipline.
- **Evidence-based personas** — behavioural clusters first, distinguishing variables second, profile
  last. ⛔ The persona sheets in this skill must be *derived*, not imagined; an invented persona
  produces invented objections and therefore invented hooks.

**And the qual-quant loop closes here:** interviews and comments generate hypotheses (WHAT and WHY),
the ads test them at scale (HOW MANY), and a surprising number sends you back to the qualitative
source. That is exactly the diagnosis waterfall in `performance-metrics.md`, run in the other
direction.

## 6b. The look board — research you can see

Six sourced lines tell you what is true. They do not tell you what it looks like, and a concept
whose "one image" was written rather than found has been checked against nothing.

So before the concepts: **four or more real reference pictures, on disk, each with a provenance
sidecar**, under `00-brief/_look/`. `scripts/develop.py` checks the files, not the prose — a line in
the Look section that names a picture which is not there is a finding, and so is a picture with no
sidecar (`stock-sourcing.md` §9).

    pixabay_search.py -q "<the category's real subject>" --image-type photo --download 00-brief/_look/

What the board is for, in order of value:

1. **It kills concepts cheaply.** "Hands sorting olives at dawn" survives a paragraph and dies the
   moment you look at twenty photographs of hands sorting olives and see that every one of them is
   the same stock cliché. That is a five-minute kill instead of a five-hundred-credit one.
2. **It supplies the vocabulary the prompts will need.** The words that describe a picture you are
   looking at are concrete; the words that describe an imagined one are adjectives.
3. **It surfaces the §5 blockers before anything is committed** — recognisable faces, trademarks,
   distinctive property. Discovering the category's best references are all unusable is a fact you
   want at concept stage, not at delivery.

The board is not the reference set. These are pictures to think against; `_reference/` is the
sourced, cleared, one-attribute-per-file set the prompts actually bind (`stock-sourcing.md` §9b).

## 7. Where research lands, or it does not exist

Every T1+ finding must land in exactly one of:

- **the brief** — as a line under one of the six fields, or a non-goal;
- **the hook bank** — as a hook with the source phrase it came from;
- **the persona sheet** — as a behavioural attribute with its evidence;
- **`_series/DECISIONS.md`** — as a decision record, for anything T3 (`series-continuity.md`);
- **this skill** — as an episode note, if it changes how the *work* is done (`self-evolution.md`).

- **the person's own list of what mattered** — a line they do not name is deleted, and its id is listed as
  deleted so citations stay readable (§2.1b, v2.69.0).

⛔ **Research that lands nowhere gets deleted, not filed.** A findings document nobody reads is the
artifact form of the Bastardi effect: it will be consulted exactly once, at the moment it can only
distort a decision that was already sound.

## 8. What this file cannot tell you

- **Whether better briefs actually produce better ads.** The evidence is industry self-report (§1.3).
  Nobody has coded brief quality against outcomes in the one dataset that could do it.
- **A number for how deep a given piece of research should go.** The ladder in §2 is a structure built
  on four sourced traditions; the *assignment* of tiers to question types in §5 is this skill's
  judgment, not a finding.
- **Whether pre-testing a message predicts in-market performance.** Genuinely contested, and the
  platform documentation that would settle it was not readable (robots-disallowed).
- **What the major platforms document about creative research.** Their guidance pages could not be
  retrieved; treat any second-hand summary of them as SECONDARY at best.
- **Whether the 9–17 saturation band transfers from research interviews to review-mining or comment
  mining at all.** It is the best available anchor and it was not measured on this kind of source.
