# Ideation — where the angle comes from before anyone writes a line

> **Before ideating, decide how much you are allowed to research.** The depth ladder, the stopping
> rule, and the sourced six-field brief skeleton are `preproduction-research.md`. Two things from it
> govern this stage: ⛔ **do not use the creative process to work out your strategy** (a sourced
> trade-body prohibition, and the most expensive habit available in a generative pipeline), and
> **more *alternatives* is reliably good where more *background* is not** — when in doubt, spend the
> hour on a third angle rather than a fourth source on the first two.

Every other file in this skill assumes the idea exists. Stage 0 intake asks for "the one dramatic
question" or "the angle" as if it arrives with the brief. It usually doesn't — and at 30–50 videos a
month it definitively doesn't, because the constraint stops being production and becomes **having
forty things worth saying**.

This is the divergence stage. It is short, it is cheap, and skipping it is why batches converge on
the same three angles by week three.

Self-contained; sources named inline (Young, *A Technique for Producing Ideas*; Mahon, *Art
Direction*; Heath & Heath, *Made to Stick*).

## 0. Their voice first — the taste session, their material, their picture (v2.65.0)

Author, 2026-11-14: "helping the user express [their] creativity better." Two independent model reviews of this
skill named the same weakness first: the person met the work as a late approver of options the agent had already
invented. So, before any concept is written and before any research is shown, the agent **asks**, and what the
person says leads.

**Why, with sources** (`cine-preproduction-2026-11-14` research note R2, in our own words):
- AI-assisted writing comes out more alike across people, even when each person's piece gets better
  [SECONDARY · Doshi & Hauser, *Science Advances* 2024, preregistered, 293 writers].
- Showing an AI example early fixes people on that first idea and narrows what they produce
  [SECONDARY · Wadinambiarachchi et al., CHI 2024, N=60].
- An AI that asks and reflects keeps ideas varied and keeps the person's sense of ownership; one that rewrites their
  ideas loses both [SECONDARY · Maier et al., reported for CHI 2026, about 480 people, repeated with about 640 —
  read as an arXiv abstract, not peer-reviewed text].

**The taste session** (`00-brief/DEVELOPMENT.md` § Taste; `develop.py` checks it):
1. Ask **three questions at a time**, with AskUserQuestion or in chat, and let them skip any: what would make them
   proud of the film, what would make them cringe, two things they love (a film, an ad, a photo — a file, link or
   title) and what they love about each, one thing they hate and why, what must never appear, and who it is for.
2. **Write their words, in quotes.** Reflect back what you heard ("so: dry, not cosy?") and let them correct it.
   ⛔ Never rewrite their answer into tidier copy, and never put the agent's words in their mouth.
3. If they would rather not, record `skipped: "<their words>"`. A skip is their decision, and the agent then says
   it is working without their taste on file.

**Their material** (§ Their material): their own ideas (`[seed]`), stories, phrases and a moment they wish they had
made, verbatim. Every seed becomes a concept in the bank (`from: their seed`) and is argued in the adversarial rounds
exactly like the agent's — never left in the notes.

**Their picture:** at least one picture on the look board is theirs (a photo of their place, a sketch, a
screenshot), with a provenance sidecar. The stock pictures map the category's clichés; theirs sets the direction.

**The pick stays theirs** (§ Choice): show the survivors **shuffled, with the agent's recommendation last**, each with
its trade-off. They may pick one, combine two with their own twist, bring their own (write it up as a concept
first), or pick none (back to concepts). Record what they refused and why, in their words: a refusal steers every
later stage as much as a pick.

**What this cannot do:** it cannot make a person creative on demand, and the studies behind it used short text and
image tasks, not video briefs. It keeps their voice on file and in front of every later stage; whether that makes
better films is for the jobs to show.

## 1. Ideas are recombination, not invention

Young's claim, and the reason this stage is teachable rather than mystical: **an idea is a new
combination of old elements**, and the capacity to make new combinations depends on your ability to
see relationships. Nobody produces a good angle from an empty head.

His five steps, which loop rather than run once:

1. **Immersion** — gather raw material. Specific to the brief *and* general to the world.
2. **Digestion** — turn the material over. Group it, contrast it, look for the odd pairing.
3. **Incubation** — put it down and do something else. This is a step, not a delay.
4. **Illumination** — the combination arrives, usually not at the desk.
5. **Verification** — shape it, test it, and expect it to need work. A raw idea is not a concept.

**The operational consequence — the one thing to take from this:** *if you feel stuck, you are short
of materials, not short of method.* The instinct is to push harder on step 2. The fix is to go back
to step 1.

## 2. The materials — where the raw stuff actually is

For this work, specifically, in rough order of yield:

- **The objection list.** What stops people buying. Every real objection is a video. This is the
  richest source and the most under-used, because it is uncomfortable.
- **The archive.** What footage already exists. Constraint is generative: "what can we say with what
  we've got" produces sharper angles than a blank page.
- **The comments and the search box.** How people phrase the problem in their own words — which is
  also the hook (`persuasion-psychology.md` §2 and the persona sheet).
- **Support and sales conversations.** The question asked most often is a video; the question asked
  once, oddly, is often a better one.
- **The competitor set.** Not to copy — to find the thing nobody in the category is saying, which is
  where the "right kind of wrongness" lives (`brand-system.md` §7).
- **The product's own boring detail.** The specific mechanism, the number, the thing the founder
  finds too obvious to mention. Concreteness is the velcro of memory.
- **Adjacent domains.** Young's analogy step: how does a completely different category solve this
  persuasion problem? Cross-domain recombination is where the non-obvious angles come from.

## 2b. ⛔ When the category's claim set is blocked, that IS the brief

The reflex on reading a compliance file is that it costs you the good ideas. Run it the other way.

**Every category has one ad that everybody makes.** It exists because it is the most obvious
dramatisation of the category's central claim — and the central claim is usually the one under the
most regulatory pressure, precisely because everybody is making it. So the blocked list and the
convention list are **the same list**, and a brand that cannot make the conventional ad has been
handed the one thing divergence normally has to be bought: a reason not to.

Work it as three questions, in this order:

1. **What is the one ad everyone in this category makes?** Name it in a sentence, with its shot.
   If you cannot, you have not looked at enough competitor creative (§2).
2. **Which of its claims can this client actually make?** Usually few, and usually not the emotional
   payload. Write down what survives — it is almost always **terms, mechanism and materials**:
   numbers, construction, what the thing is made of, what happens if it fails.
3. **What does the surviving material make possible that the convention does not?** This is the
   creative question, and it is a real one, not a consolation.

⛔ **The convergence worth knowing about**, because it recurs: in a claims-heavy category the
constraint usually pushes toward **the object and away from the person**, and that direction is
also the right one for three unrelated reasons this skill has already established.

- **Compliance.** A person claiming an experience is an endorsement carrying the advertiser's full
  substantiation burden, and a *synthetic* person claiming one is a fake testimonial no disclosure
  cures (`compliance.md`). An object claims nothing.
- **The uncanny.** It is a **kinematics mismatch**, and it has two exits — raise motion fidelity, or
  **lower appearance realism so the human-motion prediction is never generated**
  (`motion-craft.md` §2). Away from the photoreal person is one of the two principled solutions,
  not a retreat.
- **Motion.** A person lying still is the lowest-motion subject there is, and short-and-static is
  the failure mode (`motion-craft.md` §3). Materials at macro are the opposite: they compress,
  recover, deform, pour. **And they carry anticipation, action and settle physically** — the three
  things a generative model never produces unprompted (§1 there) become inevitable because the
  object actually behaves that way.

So the blocked brief, the AI pipeline's weakest subject, and the category's most-copied shot are all
the same shot. Losing it is not a cost.

**Keep or break, said once per concept (v2.69.0).** At T1 and up every concept carries one line:
`category: <the usual ad here> · ours: keep | break — <how our first three seconds differ>`. One sentence is cheap
and makes the convention a decision instead of an accident; the concept board shows it beside the concept.

**The three ad cards, only when the convention IS the decision** (the person is choosing whether to meet or break
it, or the claims are blocked as above): three real ads, each a card in our own words — the claim, the proof, the
first three seconds' image, the convention it follows. A **person** opens the ad libraries in a browser (the
public ad libraries exist for exactly this); the `cine-researcher` agent never downloads media (its rule 3) and
writes the cards from what the person saved. Frames are optional, stay on the studio's machine, never go in a board
sent to a client, and are deleted once the card is written. No shot counts, no account lists, no gate.

**One more move, where the claim itself is contested across the whole category:**
⛔ **the risk reversal is the message.** Where nobody can prove their claim and every competitor is
asserting one anyway, a brand that says *"don't take my word for it — here is how long you have to
disagree with me"* wins the argument by refusing to have it. It is the `reason-why` and the skeptic
structure (`persuasion-psychology.md` §2) pointed at the category's own credibility problem, it
needs no substantiation because it is a **term, not a claim**, and it is usually the one thing on
the brief nobody has bothered to dramatise.

## 3. Diverge before you converge

The failure mode is arriving with one idea and spending the session defending it.

- **Generate to a quota, not to satisfaction.** Ten concepts, one line each, before evaluating any of
  them. The first three are the obvious ones; the interesting material starts around six, when the
  obvious moves are used up.
- **Nothing is judged during generation.** Evaluating while generating kills the weird ones, and the
  weird ones are the reason for the exercise.
- **Deliberately generate bad ones.** The worst-possible-idea round is not a joke — inverting a bad
  idea is a reliable route to a good one, and it breaks the habitual pattern.
- **Then kill fast.** Convergence should be brutal and quick. Most of the ten are correctly binned.

## 4. The angle grid — structured divergence for volume

Free association doesn't scale to a monthly batch. This does:

```
        persona  ×  objection  ×  proof type  ×  emotional register
```

- **Persona** — who is speaking or being spoken to (`assets/persona-brief-template.md`).
- **Objection** — the specific thing stopping *this* persona.
- **Proof type** — demonstration, comparison, before/after, testimonial, mechanism explanation,
  third-party evidence, founder honesty.
- **Register** — funny, deadpan, warm, urgent, serious/trust-building.

Three personas × four objections × four proof types × two registers is 96 cells. **Most are empty or
absurd — that's the point.** You are looking for the dozen that are alive, and the grid finds them
faster than staring at a brief. It also guarantees tonal range rather than hoping for it, which is
usually an explicit client ask.

**Fill the grid against the hook taxonomy, not instead of it** (`modes/ugc-volume.md` §5): the cell
gives you the *content*, the taxonomy gives you the *opening move*.

## 5. Concept, not idea — what leaves this stage

A one-line idea is not yet buildable. A concept has five parts, and it fits on four lines:

```
FOR      <persona, in one clause>
WHO      <the objection or the moment>
WE SHOW  <the single visual or demonstration the whole thing turns on>
SO THAT  <what they think or feel differently>
PROOF    <the one specific, substantiable thing that makes it credible>
```

If "we show" is abstract, there is no video yet — just a message. **The concept must name the
picture**, because the picture is what gets generated and what carries the memory.

## 6. Testing a concept before it costs anything

Cheap gates, in order. Each one kills concepts for a few minutes of work rather than a few hundred
credits.

- **The four-frame test** (Mahon). Can the idea be told in about four frames — opening, closing, and
  two for the middle? **If not, it is probably too complicated**, and a fuller storyboard will hide
  that rather than reveal it.
- **The one-sentence test.** Say the concept in one sentence to someone outside the project. If they
  restate it back correctly, it survives. If they restate a *different* idea, that's the idea you
  actually have.
- **The SUCCESs bar** (Heath & Heath). Simple, Unexpected, Concrete, Credible, Emotional, Story —
  **three of six is the working bar.** Under two rarely survives contact with a feed.
- **The "so what" pass.** Say the concept, then answer "so what?" three times. If you run out of
  answers by the second, it's a fact, not an angle.
- **The substitution test.** Swap in a competitor's name. If the concept still works, it isn't about
  this brand and it won't build anything.
- **The compliance pre-read.** Does the concept *require* a claim you can't substantiate? Kill it now
  — it is much cheaper here than after a batch is scripted (`compliance.md`).

## 7. At volume: run a concept bank, not a monthly panic

- **Capture continuously, in one place.** Ideas arrive during production, not during ideation
  sessions. A concept bank with the five-line format above, added to whenever something surfaces.
- **Bank concepts, spend them monthly.** The batch plan draws from the bank; the bank is refilled
  from §2's materials. Generating a month's angles cold, monthly, is the thing that produces
  sameness.
- **Track what shipped and what happened.** A concept that produced a winner tells you which *cell*
  of the grid is live for this brand — that is a far more reusable finding than the individual video,
  and it feeds the hook bank (`modes/ugc-volume.md` §8).
- **Retire the exhausted cells.** An angle that has run four times is done, even if it worked. That
  is creative fatigue, and it shows up in the metrics before it shows up in your judgement.
