# DEVELOPMENT — acme-spring-tuneup (sample)

## Taste
- Proud of: "a video my regulars would forward to a friend".
- Would hate: "stock-footage cyclists in lycra smiling at nothing".
- Their phrase to keep: "back on the road by the weekend".

## Their material
- Phone photos of the workshop bench and the chalkboard by the door (supplied by the client, cleared for use).

## Research
- Decides the hook: the searches people type in March are "bike squeaks" and "flat tyre fix", not "bike service".
- Decides the proof: the shop's own 48-hour turnaround is the one checkable claim.

## Audience voice
- "It's been sitting in the hallway since November."
- "I keep meaning to pump the tyres."

## Look
- Four reference pictures on disk (the client's own photos), daylight through the shop window, chalk and steel.

## Concepts
### C1 · The hallway bike
premise: the bike in the hallway "watches" its owner leave by bus every morning; spring arrives; the owner books.
### C2 · The squeak
premise: a squeak follows the rider through a city morning until the shop silences it.
### C3 · Chalkboard countdown
premise: the shop's chalkboard counts down 48 hours while the bike is fixed.
### C4 · Pump in hand
premise: the owner tries to fix it alone, gives up laughing, books.
### C5 · First ride back (the client's phrase)
premise: "back on the road by the weekend" — the Saturday ride, then rewind to the booking.

## Adversarial round 1 · the bored scroller
killed: C3 — a chalkboard is not a hook in the first second.
killed: C4 — the joke lands at second 12; the scroll is gone by then.

## Adversarial round 2 · the skeptical buyer
killed: C2 — "silences the squeak" promises a result the shop cannot show in 30 seconds.
kept: C1 (the hallway is where the audience's bike actually is), C5 (the client's own phrase, the weekend payoff).

## Choice
The person picked **C1, with C5's line as the ending**: "Back on the road by the weekend." (shown shuffled, the
recommendation last; their words recorded with the approval).

## Hooks
1. (object) The bike in the hallway, a coat hung over the handlebars.
2. (sound) A tyre hissing flat in a quiet hallway.
3. (line) "It's been sitting here since November."
